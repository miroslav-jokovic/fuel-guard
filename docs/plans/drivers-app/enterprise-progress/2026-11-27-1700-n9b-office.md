# 2026-11-27 — S1 / T1 N-9, second half (N-9b): what the office sees after Send

N-9a (#2713, merged) gave the driver's API "Got it" and the McLeod stop facts. N-9b gives the office
the other side of the same facts. With it, T1 "## N-9" is complete.

## What N-9b changes

- **A dispatch now carries what happened to it** (`LoadDispatchSummary`): `acknowledgements` (who said
  "Got it" to this dispatch), `seats` (who it shows the load to: the driver, plus McLeod's co-driver when
  the dispatch names McLeod's driver), `reachable` (`no_app_login` / `uncovered_in_mcleod` / `closed`),
  `seatNote` (McLeod seats somebody else on the load) and `lastContactAt`. All are derived on each read
  by `dispatchFacts.ts`; nothing is stored.
- **Words the office sees:**
  - The board and the load page: "… · Seen 11/27/2026 9:14 AM". For a team: "Seen by Luis Ortega …;
    Dana Kelly not yet".
  - The load page: "Not in Dixon's app — no app login", the McLeod seat note, and "Driver's phone last
    reached us at …". The quiet-phone line shows when the phone has been quiet for over 60 minutes on a
    pending_approval / approved / in_transit load that reaches an app (D-MK22). It no longer shows "no
    text was sent" beside an app dispatch.
  - The Dispatch drawer says before Send that an uncovered load reaches nobody, and gives the seat note.
- **Exceptions** gains three kinds:
  - `late_evidence`: the 0661 flag, naming the driver and the stop.
  - `dispatch_not_visible`: an open McLeod load whose current dispatch reaches nobody. Acknowledging it
    holds only until the load is sent again.
  - `stop_mirror_refused`: one row per load (the newest), closed by the mirror's next clean apply.
- **One rule in one place.** `driverHasAppLogin`, `dispatchReachable`, `dispatchSeatNote` and
  `dispatchSeats` are in the shared contract. The Send's channel choice, the preview, the load page and
  the "New load" push all read them. The push used to restate the O-3 seat rule by hand.
- `lastAppCheckIns` (messaging) reads `device_push_tokens.last_seen_at`. The app refreshes it on every
  launch. Loads reads it through messaging's index rather than reading messaging's table itself.

## Where the build differs from T1's text, and why

1. **The last-contact time is attached to the dispatch summary, not computed in `detail.ts` and on the
   board row separately.** Both already read `dispatchesByLoad`, so one derivation serves both, and
   `DispatchLoadsPage.vue` stays unedited (T1's budget note).
2. **Last contact counts the driver's events on THIS load**, not on every load. The board reads facts
   for every open load at once and the load page for one. A driver-wide maximum would make the two pages
   disagree, and `payload->>driver_id` has no index. A phone that does nothing on the load still counts:
   its app check-in is the other half.
3. **`seats` is a new field.** T1 asks for the team form ("who has seen it and who has not") but does not
   say where the second seat's name comes from. The server derives it with the same rule 0655 applies.
4. **Late evidence is read with no paging of the existing reads.** The two existing `load_events` reads
   stay as they are: T1 says T-5 pages them (D-MK32). The new reads page from the start.

## Known limits (recorded, not routed around)

- Exceptions look back 30 days. If someone acknowledged a `dispatch_not_visible` more than 30 days ago,
  it reappears. Today that cannot happen: no `load_dispatches` row has ever been written in production.
- On production 2026-11-27, 194 of 195 active drivers have no app login. Every Send to one of them opens
  a `dispatch_not_visible` exception until somebody acknowledges it. That is what T1 asks for ("a sent
  load nobody can see is an open exception"). The D-MK34 go-live briefing should tell dispatchers it
  will happen.
