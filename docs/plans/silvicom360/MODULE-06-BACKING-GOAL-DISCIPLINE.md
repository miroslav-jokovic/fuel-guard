## 1. Outcome and scope

Module 6 turns the backing portion of **Verify Before Moving** into a repeatable prevention and control discipline. The source outcome is to reduce backing events through setup, walk-around, spotters, and stop-and-check habits. The proposed lesson therefore begins before reverse gear is selected: eliminate avoidable backing, select a safer setup, inspect the actual path, establish any required spotter communication, and move only while the driver continues to confirm the changing path.

The instructional aim is not “look once, then back.” The proposed behavior is to:

1. avoid or reduce backing when a safer forward option is practical and authorized;
2. stop and secure the vehicle under the approved company procedure before leaving the cab;
3. walk the actual path and inspect ground-level, side, rear, overhead, and worksite hazards;
4. set up for the greatest practical visibility and simplest path;
5. establish one approved communication plan when a spotter is required or used;
6. back slowly enough to remain in control while using the views approved for that equipment;
7. stop immediately when a critical view, reference, clearance, or spotter contact is lost, the path changes, or the driver is uncertain;
8. use GOAL again, pull forward, reposition, request control of the area, or abandon the maneuver as the approved situation requires; and
9. finish, secure, and confirm the vehicle's final position without treating dock contact or arrival as proof that every condition is safe.

For this document, **GOAL** is provisionally expanded as **Get Out and Look** because that wording is explicit in current 49 CFR Part 380. The source Silvicom360 plan uses “GOAL” but does not define it. The company must still approve the exact learner wording and operational procedure. A slogan is not a complete method: the course must define when leaving the cab is safe, how the vehicle is secured, what the driver examines, when the observation becomes stale, and what to do when getting out would itself create exposure.

A walk-around before movement and GOAL during a maneuver are related but not interchangeable. The first establishes the initial path and site conditions. A repeated GOAL refreshes information after a changed setup, lost reference, unexpected obstruction, movement near a critical clearance, or any other approved trigger. Returning to the cab does not freeze the scene; people, vehicles, doors, equipment, and site controls can change before reverse movement begins.

Digital completion can document knowledge, sequencing, hazard recognition, and stop/proceed/recheck decisions. It cannot prove physical backing skill, safe cab exit, correct mirror use, spatial control, spotter teamwork, or performance in a representative commercial vehicle. Those behaviors remain a separate Silvicom360 Road Check or qualified behind-the-wheel evaluation under an approved observation standard.

## 4. Learner design

### 4.1 Audience

The provisional audience is an authenticated company driver assigned the Silvicom360 core or targeted refresher. That is not yet enough detail for backing content. Before scripting, segment the audience at least by:

- current experienced driver versus entry-level driver;
- CDL class and endorsement as applicable;
- straight truck, tractor-semitrailer, pickup/van, bus, or specialized equipment;
- single versus multiple trailer;
- conventional versus cab-over and left-hand versus right-hand drive where relevant;
- mirror-only, camera-equipped, proximity-equipped, or mixed visibility package;
- terminal/dock, customer delivery, roadway, parking, construction/work-zone, or other operation;
- driver-only versus driver-plus-spotter responsibility; and
- language and accessibility needs.

A learner outside the validated vehicle/site profile must not receive a visual presented as their exact procedure. A common concepts lesson may use multiple profiles if every difference that changes the action is identified.

### 4.2 Prerequisites

Before Module 6 release, verify that the learner has access to approved instruction or policy for:

- company stop/park/secure and safe cab entry/exit;
- assigned vehicle controls, mirrors, camera/monitor, proximity system, alarms, and pre-operation checks;
- direct and indirect view limitations;
- full-vehicle path, articulation, rear overhang, and clearance;
- site rules, PPE, pedestrian controls, restricted areas, and reporting;
- horn, flasher, radio, and hand-signal use where applicable;
- incident/contact stop-and-report procedure; and
- emergency escalation and supervisor/contact channels.

If any prerequisite is absent, Module 6 must either include an approved bounded version or block the affected lesson path. A quiz must not test an unpublished procedure.

### 4.3 Proposed measurable learning objectives

After the digital module, the learner should be able to:

1. choose a practical forward or pull-through alternative over avoidable backing when the approved scenario permits;
2. identify the facts needed before leaving the cab and before beginning reverse movement;
3. distinguish initial walk-around, GOAL, in-cab observation, pull-up/reposition, and abort;
4. map a backing path across ground, sides, rear, overhead, moving-traffic, pedestrian, and equipment zones;
5. explain when a previous path check has become stale;
6. select a setup that simplifies the move and preserves the best available view for the represented profile;
7. identify when a spotter is required or useful under the approved procedure;
8. recognize compliant and noncompliant spotter communication, position, attention, and loss-of-contact behavior;
9. interpret mirrors, cameras, alarms, and sensors within their verified limits;
10. choose stop, GOAL, pull forward, reposition, request assistance/control, or abandon when a critical fact is unknown or changes; and
11. distinguish digital knowledge completion from observed backing proficiency and regulated ELDT/CDL certification.

The Road Check may separately evaluate observable execution. Digital objectives must not claim physical skill.

### 4.5 Proposed process model

The following **A-S-S-I-S-T** model is a planning aid only; the acronym and sequence require M6-D05–D17 approval:

1. **Avoid** unnecessary backing and choose a simpler authorized route or pull-through position.
2. **Secure and survey**: stop under the approved procedure, inspect the vehicle/site/path, and identify changing hazards.
3. **Set up** for the simplest practical path, greatest usable view, and adequate clearance.
4. **Inform and coordinate**: establish intent, site control, and the designated spotter/signals when required.
5. **Slowly move while observing** the complete path with approved views; correct early and use pull-ups as needed.
6. **Terminate movement on uncertainty**: stop on lost contact/reference/view, path change, alarm, distraction, person entry, or clearance doubt; GOAL/re-plan before resuming.

The final step is completion: place and secure the vehicle under the approved procedure, confirm final position, and report hazards, defects, near misses, or suspected contact as required. The mnemonic must be discarded if it makes the procedure harder to translate, recall, observe, or apply.

### 5.2 Legal and standards interpretation rules

1. Identify whether the learner, vehicle, work activity, site, and jurisdiction fall within the cited rule before labeling anything “required.”
2. Distinguish a regulation, regulatory appendix, agency guidance, model manual, company policy, site rule, and proposed instructional design.
3. Have qualified legal/compliance review current federal, state, provincial/local, customer-site, and company requirements for the selected launch profile.
4. Preserve source title, issuing body, URL, access date, publication/update date, section, and quoted/paraphrased status in the claim ledger.
5. Recheck current eCFR and selected jurisdiction/site requirements immediately before script approval and release.
6. Do not convert OSHA construction guidance into an all-fleet mandate. When the construction context is included, identify the exact applicable standard and State Plan differences.
7. Do not present ELDT curriculum overlap as ELDT delivery. That requires the applicable curriculum, qualified provider/instructors, representative vehicle/range/public-road work, assessments, records, and Training Provider Registry obligations.
8. Do not make a crash-reduction or incident-reduction claim from training completion alone.

### 5.3 Claim rules

Every learner-facing factual claim must have:

- a stable claim ID;
- exact approved wording;
- source and section/page;
- scope: audience, vehicle, equipment, site, jurisdiction, and conditions;
- classification: law, guidance, manufacturer limit, company policy, site policy, or instructional proposal;
- owner and qualified reviewer;
- approval date and review/expiration trigger;
- linked script, scene, assessment, Road Check, and translation usage; and
- supersession history.

Do not publish unsupported absolutes such as:

- “backing accidents are always preventable”;
- “GOAL guarantees a clear path”;
- “one spotter makes backing safe”;
- “the camera eliminates blind spots”;
- “backup alarms keep pedestrians out of the path”;
- “always get out,” when exiting could expose the driver to traffic or site hazards;
- “never blind-side back,” unless that is the approved policy and exceptions are defined;
- “one mile per hour,” or any fixed speed without an approved basis; or
- “passing the module proves backing competence.”

## 8. Script and production standards

### 8.1 Wording

- Use observable descriptions: “the spotter is no longer visible in the approved mirror,” not “the spotter disappeared.”
- Name the driver, vehicle profile, maneuver, site, path zone, and decision time.
- Distinguish “initial walk-around,” “GOAL,” “in-cab check,” “pull-up,” “reposition,” and “abort.”
- Say “unknown” when occupancy or clearance is not established.
- Explain that GOAL obtains information; it does not keep a changing site clear after the driver returns.
- State that the driver remains responsible for stopping and controlling the vehicle under company policy even when assisted.
- Never call a bystander a spotter merely because they are nearby.
- Do not call an area “clear” based only on a mirror, camera, sensor, alarm, spotter signal, or prior observation.
- Avoid test-centric penalties for safety actions. Operational GOAL and pull-ups must follow company procedure, not a skills-test scoring limit.
- Do not encourage checklist reading, phone use, filming, or lesson interaction during vehicle operation.

### 8.2 Vehicle, site, and path integrity

Every depicted maneuver must record:

- vehicle make/model or validated representative class, body, dimensions, wheelbase, rear overhang, steering geometry, load state, trailer count/type/dimensions, and articulation;
- driver seating/camera position and direct-view fields;
- mirror type, placement, adjustment basis, field of view, distortion, and known unavailable areas;
- camera/monitor, sensor, radar/sonar, alarm, and warning configuration if shown;
- starting position, intended path, final position, lane/aisle/dock geometry, grade, surface, markings, barriers, lighting, weather, and access points;
- pedestrian and equipment routes, traffic controls, door/gate/dock state, and who controls them;
- measured versus illustrative clearance/path overlays; and
- the qualified SME/site/equipment reviewers and source evidence.

Do not reuse a path overlay from another vehicle configuration. Do not depict a spotter inside the intended/swept path or an unreviewed crush zone. Do not imply exact clearance from uncalibrated lenses.

### 8.3 Temporal and multi-view integrity

- Synchronize mirror, camera, exterior, spotter, and overhead views to a documented common time base.
- Record frame rate, timestamp source, alignment tolerance, lens processing, crop, zoom, playback speed, and any reconstruction.
- Do not combine a clear camera frame from one moment with a clear mirror frame from another and present them as simultaneous.
- Preserve changing people/vehicles/equipment across cuts or label reenactment/animation.
- Separate the learner's available information from answer-reveal overlays.
- Have a second reviewer reproduce every known/unknown/stale/lost-contact classification from the source assets.

### 8.4 Spotter and filming safety

- Demonstrate signals without vehicle movement before filming a live maneuver.
- Use a closed, controlled area with a written movement plan, qualified operator, designated director, exclusion zones, emergency stop, and communications check.
- No actor, crew member, spotter, or equipment may enter the active backing path to make a scene look realistic.
- Never direct a spotter to walk backward in the vehicle path or stand between the vehicle and a fixed object.
- Stop production on lost communication, unplanned entry, equipment issue, weather/lighting change, or uncertain clearance.
- Prefer validated animation/simulation or composite graphics for hazardous failures.
- Real incidents require legal, privacy, labor, dignity, and educational-necessity review; do not imply blame from incomplete footage.

### 8.5 Audio, captions, description, and transcript

- Caption narration and meaningful sound, including backup alarms, horn, radio calls, stop calls, and warning tones.
- Provide a transcript containing spoken text, important labels, vehicle movement, view changes, spotter signals, warning states, hazards, and answer rationales.
- Provide audio description or a structured equivalent for path geometry, articulation, spotter position, changing occupancy, and clearance.
- Do not rely on color, arrow direction, sound, flashing, hand motion, or screen position alone.
- Define signal meaning in text; do not expect the learner to infer gestures from video.
- Keep audio description from masking emergency or equipment sounds.
- Verify synchronization at every supported playback rate and after localization.

### 8.6 Accessible backing exercises

Every visual/spatial exercise must provide an equivalent that preserves the same decision:

- a timestamped table of vehicle, person, equipment, spotter, and access changes;
- an ordered path description for power unit, trailer, rear overhang, and final location;
- a zone table for direct view, each mirror, camera, sensor state, and unknown areas;
- signal names and meanings in text;
- labeled stills with meaningful reading order and sufficient zoom;
- patterns/text in addition to color or animation;
- pause, replay, and review without score penalty;
- keyboard and screen-reader operability where applicable; and
- no scoring based on rapid response, precise dragging, vision-only target selection, head/eye movement, or spoken speed.

The accessible version must not reveal an obstruction, path change, or correct answer earlier than the visual version.

Minimum bank counts are planning targets. Served count, weighting, pass score, attempts, feedback timing, remediation, and version-transition rules require M6-D18 approval.

### 9.3 Item-writing rules

- Supply every fact required: vehicle, site, path, current view, spotter state, equipment state, and approved policy.
- Use “What is the safest approved next action now?” when the answer depends on a changed condition.
- Reward stop, GOAL, pull-up, reposition, area control, assistance, or abort when critical facts are unknown.
- Never make “continue slowly and hope the view returns” the correct response.
- Do not treat camera silence, alarm operation, a spotter's earlier signal, or an earlier walk-around as current clearance.
- Do not test a hand signal before it is taught in both visual and text form.
- Do not require exact distance judgment from uncalibrated images.
- Use plausible distractors tied to a documented misconception.
- Explain why each rejected option is unsafe or unsupported at that moment.
- Validate translated spatial, modal, and safety wording independently.
- Keep knowledge score separate from Road Check, incident review, employment discipline, and ELDT records.

### 9.4 Road Check boundary and proposed observations

After M6-D18, a qualified observer may rate approved behaviors such as whether the driver:

- identifies and uses a practical forward alternative when available;
- stops and secures the vehicle correctly before leaving the cab;
- completes the approved path/site inspection and identifies relevant changes;
- sets up for a simple, visible, controlled path;
- briefs and uses a spotter correctly when required;
- refuses ambiguous/conflicting signals and stops on lost contact;
- uses approved direct, mirror, and assistance-system information without overreliance;
- backs at the approved controlled speed and corrects early;
- stops when a reference, view, clearance, or path condition becomes unknown;
- uses GOAL, pull-ups, repositioning, or abort appropriately;
- maintains safe separation from people, vehicles, equipment, and fixed objects; and
- completes, secures, confirms, and reports under company procedure.

The rubric must define observable Meets / Coach / Critical anchors, automatic-stop conditions, observer positioning, permitted coaching, maneuver/site/profile, weather/lighting limits, signature/acknowledgment, disagreement handling, recheck, and record retention. Do not ask an observer to stand in an unsafe position to score a behavior.

### 9.5 Pilot measurement design

Measure separately:

- item difficulty, discrimination, distractor selection, omission, and completion time;
- accessibility and language parity;
- frequency/reasons for stop, GOAL, pull-up, and abort choices in scenarios;
- Road Check agreement between trained observers;
- learner confidence versus verified knowledge/observation;
- platform failures, offline/resume behavior, caption use, and support requests; and
- only after an approved event taxonomy/data source exists, operational trends with exposure and human review.

Do not infer crash reduction from a small or uncontrolled pre/post change. An increase in GOAL or pull-ups may reflect safer behavior, not worse skill; the program owner must approve interpretation before publishing a KPI.

## 14. Rollout, monitoring, and rollback

### 14.1 Rollout

1. Internal SME/accessibility review with synthetic records.
2. Controlled observer calibration in a safe representative environment.
3. Small pilot across selected vehicle/site/language/accessibility profiles.
4. Limited real assignments with support and stop authority.
5. Broader rollout only after approval of pilot evidence.

Do not enable Module 6 globally merely because media uploaded successfully or the quiz passes automated tests.

### 14.2 Monitoring

Monitor:

- media start/resume/completion and integrity errors;
- item anomalies and misconception patterns;
- accessibility, localization, and support issues;
- Road Check scheduling, result distribution, rechecks, and observer agreement;
- assignment disputes, applicability mistakes, and profile mismatch;
- reported confusing signals, unsafe demonstrations, equipment mismatches, or site conflicts;
- approved near-miss/incident reviews for curriculum relevance, without automatic causality; and
- regulatory, manufacturer, fleet, site, and policy changes.

Predefine the owner and threshold for content freeze, assignment pause, item retirement, profile exclusion, retraining, and full rollback.

### 14.3 Rollback

- Disable new M06 assignments without deleting historical evidence.
- Revoke delivery of the affected content version while preserving audit history.
- Retire invalid items/signals/profiles and publish a new immutable version after approval.
- Preserve prior attempts, completion, Road Checks, acknowledgments, and exact version references.
- Notify affected learners/observers only through approved channels with minimum necessary detail.
- Reassign/recheck only under an approved impact analysis.
- Never rewrite a historical result to make it appear that the learner completed revised content.
