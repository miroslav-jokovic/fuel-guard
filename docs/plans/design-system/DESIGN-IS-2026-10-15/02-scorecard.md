# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Operations uses a familiar equal-card dashboard while Fleet overview already has a more purposeful editorial composition (E1, E2).
   Justification: It applies a standard pattern without a clear interaction improvement of its own.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Every actionable count opens its filtered queue in one click, but the reader must scan ten cards and 33 rows to determine today's priorities (E1, E7).
   Justification: The task is supported directly, but simultaneous choice and weak priority add avoidable work.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Tokens are coherent, but equal-height rows create conspicuous dead space and ten repeated card anatomies flatten the page (E2).
   Justification: One jarring layout violation outweighs the otherwise consistent palette and typography.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: HOS, GPS, OOS, generic “Review” doors, and route-label mismatches require interpretation (E3, E5).
   Justification: More than two controls or labels are unclear without contextual reading.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Card chrome is quiet, but 33 repeated purple arrow doors and ten equal card frames compete with the operational content (E2, E4).
   Justification: The chrome is restrained individually but too visible in aggregate.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Counts and live claims are generally grounded, but “today” spans mixed windows and “Last 30 days” is implemented as 31 inclusive dates (E3).
   Justification: There is no deception, but multiple label-to-behavior mismatches prevent a perfect score.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Stable tokens and primitives are durable; the homogeneous bento-card wall is the one dated marker (E2, E7).
   Justification: The visual language is mostly durable, but the composition reads as a trend rather than an information hierarchy.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Focus, empty, and disabled states exist; loading is only a dash, card errors are absent, and compact DoorLink targets are about 20 px high (E4, E5).
   Justification: Two to three important states/details remain rough or missing.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Initial JS is about 767 KB gzip, the map chunk is loaded off-map, and the page is estimated at 23 data requests; idle animation is zero and dark mode is honored (E6).
   Justification: The bundle sits in the 500 KB–2 MB rubric band and performs unnecessary work.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 0/3
    Evidence: Ten equal cards, 33 repeated door links, and at least 132 row-level semantic items dominate the page (E1, E4).
    Justification: The surface is dominated by duplicated affordances and undifferentiated containers.

Total: **13/30**
