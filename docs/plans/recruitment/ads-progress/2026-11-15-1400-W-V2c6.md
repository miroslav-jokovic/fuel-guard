**2026-11-15 · Track W · V2c-6 — a server can plan a campaign**

`CampaignWorkers.plan` is filled. A campaign is now planned before its ads are made — **for a carrier that
switched the planning agent on**.

Built:

- **The planner in the shipped workers** (`orchestrator/workers.ts`): the planning step, handed recruiting's lead
  counts by the two composition roots (the `ads_step` job handler, and `schedulers.ts` for the orchestrator's
  tick). The tick reads no lead; it is handed the counts only so its workers are the same set the job builds —
  a tick with no planner would never queue a campaign that is in `planning`.
- **`plansFor` — the carrier's switch decides (Q-APX6, open; built as (a)).** Asked once, at `draft`. Off, never
  set, or no settings saved: the campaign takes the road it took before planning existed. A campaign already in
  `planning` is planned to the end even if the switch was turned off since.
- **A planned campaign waits on the Strategist at Launch.** The launch gate's table has a second list,
  `meta:plannedstill`, derived from `meta:still` plus the Strategist; the door reads whether the campaign
  carries a plan. A campaign that was never planned is not held back by an agent it never used.

**What this changes on a server today: nothing.** Ads are off on every server, production has no saved ads
settings, and the switch is off wherever settings exist. The first planned campaign will be on staging, with the
simulator, once ads are switched on there — that walk (create a campaign, read the plan, edit it, approve it,
watch the ads made) is owed and has not been done, because it needs `ADS_ENABLED` on staging.

**Still not built: the ads are not yet made FROM the plan.** A planned and approved campaign composes exactly
as an unplanned one does — from the seeded preset draw. The plan is read, edited, approved and stored on the
campaign, and nothing downstream reads its angles yet. That is the next step, and until it lands planning adds a
review step without changing a single ad. Said plainly so nobody reads "planning is on" as "ads are planned".
